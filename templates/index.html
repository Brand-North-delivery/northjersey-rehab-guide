A better way to compare

“Best” depends on what you need. We explain trade-offs so you can shortlist with confidence. The right center is the one that can safely treat your substance use, mental health, medical needs and recovery goals—not simply the one with the newest building.

Level of care first

Detox, residential, partial care, intensive outpatient and outpatient are different products. Matching the level to the person matters more than the brand.

Licensing and accreditation

New Jersey licenses substance use and mental health separately, through DMHAS rather than a single agency. Accreditation by CARF or The Joint Commission is voluntary and worth asking about, and every North Jersey center displays the seals differently.

What is actually on site

Several centers list services delivered by referral rather than in house. Ask which parts happen at the Bergen County address you will actually be driving to, and which happen somewhere else in northern New Jersey.

Editorial review

We assessed each provider’s publicly available information across four practical dimensions, weighted equally at 25 percent each.

Clinical clarity

Are the programs, schedules and clinical staffing described specifically enough to know what you are buying?

Medical support

What medical and psychiatric services are named, and are they delivered on site or by referral?

Program depth

How many levels of care are offered, and what therapies and specialty tracks are documented?

Continuing care

What happens after the program ends—step-down, alumni support, family involvement?

Our scores measure the clarity and breadth of publicly available information—not treatment outcomes or patient satisfaction. They are not patient reviews, clinical outcome ratings or guarantees. A center may deliver excellent care and publish very little about it. Always confirm current licensing, clinical staff, costs and admission suitability directly with the center.

The care compass

Ten questions for each of ten situations. The question is the asset — each one is written so a center’s answer is checkable.

Your care compass · Alcohol rehab

Ten questions before you choose

For anyone whose primary substance is alcohol. Alcohol withdrawal is one of the few that can kill you, so the first questions here are medical, not therapeutic. A polished website cannot tell you whether a program is clinically right for you. These questions can.

01Will you assess my withdrawal risk before I choose a level of care?

This should happen on the first phone call, not at intake. Alcohol withdrawal can cause seizures and delirium tremens. A center that books you into outpatient without asking how much you drink daily and when you last drank is skipping the step that matters most.

02Do you use a withdrawal scale, and which one?

CIWA-Ar is the standard. Ask who administers it and how often. If nobody can name an instrument, withdrawal is being eyeballed.

03If I need medical detox, do you provide it here or send me elsewhere?

Of the six centers in this guide, BlueCrest and Boca Englewood list detox at the location. Others arrange it through partners, which means clinical responsibility changes hands during the riskiest phase.

04Which medications do you use for alcohol use disorder?

Naltrexone, acamprosate and disulfiram are the three approved options. A center that offers none of them is running a talk-therapy-only program, which is a legitimate choice but you should know it.

05Who prescribes and monitors those medications?

Ask for the role and the credential, not a name. A psychiatric nurse practitioner or physician should be involved, and you should know how often you will actually see them.

06How many hours a week is the program, and on which days?

Partial care and intensive outpatient differ by a factor of two or three. Program names are marketing; hours are the product.

07What happens if I drink during treatment?

The answer reveals the program's philosophy. Immediate discharge and no consequence at all are both warning signs. You want a stated, graduated clinical response.

08Is my liver function being checked, and by whom?

Long-term heavy drinking damages the liver silently. Ask whether bloodwork is part of intake and who reviews it.

09What does aftercare look like at three months, not three weeks?

Most relapse happens after the program ends. Ask what specifically exists at 90 days and whether it costs extra.

10Can I keep working while I attend?

If you need to, say so before you are assessed. Evening intensive outpatient exists at Valley Spring and North Jersey Recovery Center; most programs run during business hours.

Your care compass · Drug rehab

Ten questions before you choose

For substances other than alcohol, where the withdrawal risk profile and the medication options differ sharply by drug. The right questions depend on what you are actually using, so start by being specific with the center rather than saying "drugs."

01Which substances do you actually treat most often?

Every center says it treats everything. Ask what their caseload is really made of. A program that mostly sees opioid clients runs differently from one that mostly sees stimulant clients.

02Is withdrawal from my substance medically dangerous?

Benzodiazepine withdrawal can be fatal and requires a supervised taper. Opioid withdrawal is rarely fatal but is the reason most people stop trying. Stimulant withdrawal is mostly psychological. The answer changes the level of care you need.

03Do you offer medication-assisted treatment, and which medications?

ChoicePoint publishes buprenorphine, methadone and naltrexone by name. Specificity is a good sign; "we offer MAT" without naming anything is not.

04If I am on methadone or buprenorphine, can I continue it here?

Some programs require tapering off before admission. That is a legitimate model and also a dealbreaker for many people. Ask before you are admitted, not after.

05Do you drug test, how often, and what happens with a positive result?

Ask for the written policy. Testing without a stated clinical response is surveillance rather than treatment.

06Do you prescribe or provide naloxone to clients and families?

For anyone with opioid involvement this should be automatic, and family members should be trained to use it.

07What is your policy if I leave against medical advice?

Ask whether you can return, and how quickly. A program that treats leaving as permanent disqualification is not built around how relapse actually works.

08How do you handle a client who is still using at intake?

"You have to be clean to start" is a real answer, and it means you need somewhere else first. Better to know now.

09Who on staff has addiction-specific credentials?

Ask for LCADC, CADC or an equivalent. A licensed therapist without addiction training is not the same thing.

10What is the plan if this level of care turns out to be wrong in week one?

Good programs reassess early. Ask what the step-up path is and whether it means changing providers.

Your care compass · Medical detox

Ten questions before you choose

For anyone who may face withdrawal or needs medication and overnight observation. This is the single point where the difference between on-site and by-referral matters most, because it is where clinical responsibility can quietly change hands.

01Who performs the pre-admission assessment?

A nurse or physician should be involved before you are told which level of care you need. If the first person assessing you works in admissions rather than clinically, you are being sold, not assessed.

02Is medical cover available overnight?

Ask specifically whether a clinician is physically present overnight or on call from home. These are different products at the same price.

03Which withdrawals can be managed on site?

Alcohol and benzodiazepines carry the highest medical risk. A program should be able to say plainly which it will and will not manage in house.

04Where would I go in an emergency?

Ask for the receiving hospital by name and how far it is. Every center should have an immediate answer.

05Are detox costs included, or billed separately?

If detox happens at a partner facility, it may be billed by that facility, possibly out of network. This is one of the most common sources of surprise bills in the entire industry.

06If detox is by referral, who holds clinical responsibility during it?

IKON states plainly that detox is not provided in house. That transparency is worth something, but you still need to know who owns your care during the handoff.

07How long does detox typically last for my substance?

Ask for a range and what determines the endpoint. "It depends" without any detail means nobody has thought about your case yet.

08What happens immediately after detox ends?

The days after detox carry the highest overdose risk, because tolerance has dropped. A plan that ends at discharge is not a plan.

09Will my regular medications be continued during detox?

Psychiatric medications in particular. Ask who reviews your current prescriptions and when.

10Is the detox facility licensed and accredited separately?

If it is a different organization, its credentials are a separate question from the ones on the website you are reading.

Your care compass · Dual diagnosis

Ten questions before you choose

For people managing a substance use disorder and a mental health condition at the same time. The critical question is not whether a center says it treats both, but whether it holds the license and the staff to actually do so.

01Do you hold a separate mental health license, or only a substance use license?

New Jersey licenses these separately. Valley Spring publishes both numbers, #200887 and #70420104. A center with only a substance use license is treating your mental health condition as a secondary concern by regulatory definition.

02Can I be admitted if my primary problem is mental health, with no substance use?

Only a center with a standalone mental health license can say yes. This is the sharpest dividing line in this market and most competitors cannot cross it.

03Which psychiatric conditions do you actually treat?

Ask for the list. If eating disorders or schizophrenia spectrum disorders appear on a generic list but nobody can describe the program, they are marketing rather than clinical claims.

04Who prescribes psychiatric medication, and how often will I see them?

A prescriber you see once at intake and never again is not medication management.

05Are the two conditions treated together or in sequence?

Integrated treatment is the standard of care. Sequential treatment, where you are told to get sober first and address the trauma later, is an older model and you should know which you are buying.

06Do you use validated symptom measures, and which?

PHQ-9 for depression, GAD-7 for anxiety, PCL-5 for PTSD. Named instruments mean symptoms are being tracked rather than asked about.

07What happens if my psychiatric symptoms worsen during treatment?

Ask for the escalation path and whether it requires leaving the program.

08Is psychiatric care included in the program cost or billed separately?

Frequently separate, and frequently a different network status.

09Do you coordinate with my existing psychiatrist or therapist?

Ask whether they will actually call, and who signs the release.

10What is your experience with my specific diagnosis?

Bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder and psychotic disorders each need different competence. Generic dual diagnosis language covers all three without committing to any.

Your care compass · Trauma & PTSD

Ten questions before you choose

For people whose substance use sits on top of trauma, and for those seeking trauma treatment in its own right. Trauma-informed is a phrase almost every center uses; these questions distinguish the ones who have built something from the ones who have written a sentence.

01What does trauma-informed mean in your program specifically?

Ask for a concrete practice, not a philosophy. If the answer is entirely about atmosphere and respect, no trauma treatment is happening.

02Which trauma modalities do you deliver, and who is trained in them?

EMDR, cognitive processing therapy and prolonged exposure are the evidence-based options. Ask how many staff are certified, not whether the center offers it.

03Do you screen for trauma at intake, and with what?

The PCL-5 is standard for PTSD. Ask whether screening happens routinely or only when someone volunteers it.

04Is trauma work done in individual sessions, groups, or both?

Group-only trauma processing is a real risk for re-traumatization. Ask what happens individually.

05When in treatment does trauma work begin?

Beginning trauma processing during acute withdrawal is generally inadvisable. A center should be able to explain its sequencing.

06What happens if trauma work destabilizes me?

Ask about the stabilization plan and who is available between sessions.

07Do you offer a first responder or veteran track?

Occupational trauma has specific features. Valley Spring and IKON both publish first responder programming; ask what it actually contains beyond the label.

08Can I decline trauma work and still be in the program?

Yes should be the answer. Mandatory disclosure is a warning sign.

09How do you handle trauma disclosures in mixed groups?

Ask for the group agreement and how it is enforced.

10Is there aftercare specifically for trauma, or does it end with the program?

Trauma work rarely completes inside a 30 or 60 day episode. Ask what continues.

Your care compass · Mental health only

Ten questions before you choose

For people who do not have a substance use problem and need structured outpatient psychiatric care. This is the hardest category to shop for in New Jersey, because most facilities marketing to you are licensed primarily for addiction.

01Do you admit clients with no substance use diagnosis at all?

This is the first question and it eliminates most options immediately. It requires a standalone mental health license.

02What is your mental health license number and its expiration?

Ask for the number, then check it. Valley Spring publishes #70420104 on its site, which is unusual and easy to verify.

03Is the mental health track physically and clinically separate from the addiction track?

Ask whether groups are mixed. Sitting in a relapse-prevention group when you have never used substances is a common and demoralizing experience.

04What levels of care do you offer for mental health specifically?

Some centers offer a full ladder for addiction and only outpatient for mental health. The website often does not distinguish.

05Which conditions are within your scope, and which are not?

A center that says it treats everything is telling you it has not thought about scope. Eating disorders in particular usually require medical monitoring that outpatient licensure does not cover.

06Will I see a psychiatric prescriber, and how often?

Ask for the actual cadence, weekly or monthly, and whether it is included.

07Is my treatment billed to mental health benefits or substance use benefits?

These are separate buckets on most plans. It matters if one is exhausted or excluded.

08What is the plan if I need a higher level of care?

None of the centers in this guide offer psychiatric inpatient care. Ask where they refer and whether they have a relationship there.

09Do you provide crisis coverage outside program hours?

Ask what happens at 2am. If the answer is 988 or the emergency room, that is honest but it is also the whole answer.

10How is progress measured?

Named instruments administered on a schedule. Anything else is impression.

Your care compass · Insurance & cost

Ten questions before you choose

For everyone, because this is where the avoidable damage happens. Nobody in this market publishes prices, so the only protection is asking precise questions and getting the answers in writing before intake rather than after.

01Are you in network for my specific plan, or billing as out of network?

Not "do you take my insurance." The difference between in and out of network is frequently thousands of dollars, and both answers sound like yes on the phone.

02What is the expected out-of-pocket total, in writing, before I start?

A center that will not put a number in writing is telling you something. Ask for a range with the assumptions stated.

03Do you accept Medicaid or Medicare?

ChoicePoint states it accepts both. BlueCrest states it accepts neither. Most others do not say, which usually means no.

04How many days or sessions has my plan authorized so far?

Authorizations are often much shorter than the program length being described to you.

05What happens when the authorization runs out mid-treatment?

Ask whether you will be discharged, transitioned, or billed privately. This is the moment people get hurt.

06Is any part of my treatment plan delivered by a different provider?

Detox at a partner facility, labs, psychiatric services. Each can carry its own network status and its own bill.

07What is the self-pay rate per level of care?

Ask even if you have insurance. It tells you what the program actually costs and gives you a reference point.

08Is there a sliding scale or financial hardship policy?

Ask before the clinical conversation, not after. It is a different department and a different answer.

09Will you bill me for missed sessions?

Common and often unmentioned. Ask for the policy in writing.

10Who do I contact about a bill I do not understand?

Ask for a name and a direct number. "Billing" is not an answer.

Your care compass · Working professionals

Ten questions before you choose

For people who cannot stop working to get treatment. The scheduling question is not a convenience issue, it is the difference between completing a program and dropping out of one, and most centers run during business hours.

01Do you run a genuine evening program, and what are the exact hours?

Valley Spring publishes Monday to Friday, 6:00 to 9:00 PM. North Jersey Recovery Center also lists an evening option. Ask for start and end times, not "we have evenings."

02What happens if I miss a session for work?

Ask for the attendance policy and the make-up path before you enroll.

03Can I take FMLA for this, and will you complete the paperwork?

Treatment for a serious health condition can qualify. Ask who on staff completes employer forms and how quickly.

04What does my employer have to be told?

In most cases, nothing about the diagnosis. Ask what the center will and will not disclose, and get it in writing.

05Do you offer case management or return-to-work support?

Valley Spring publishes both. Ask what it concretely involves rather than whether it exists.

06Is telehealth available if I travel for work?

Ask whether it is equivalent to in-person attendance or counted differently.

07Do you work with employee assistance programs?

An EAP referral can change both cost and confidentiality. Ask how it is handled.

08If I hold a professional license, are there reporting obligations?

Nurses, physicians, pilots, attorneys and CDL holders all have specific monitoring programs. Ask whether the center has experience with yours.

09Will treatment appear on my insurance explanation of benefits?

If you are on a family plan, ask who receives the EOB.

10How long is the whole commitment, realistically?

Ask for weeks, not "it varies." You cannot plan around a range nobody will state.

Your care compass · Family support

Ten questions before you choose

For parents, partners and adult children trying to help someone who may not have asked for help. The questions here are about what you are entitled to know, what you can actually do, and how to prepare so that nothing stands in the way when the person becomes willing.

01What can you tell me about my family member's treatment?

Very little without a signed release, and that is the law working correctly. Ask what a release would cover and how to request one.

02Is there a family program, and is it live or recorded?

Ask who facilitates it, what their credential is, and when it starts relative to admission.

03Can I attend if my family member does not want me to?

Usually not the clinical sessions. Ask whether there is separate family education you can attend regardless.

04What should I do if they refuse treatment?

Pushing harder generally backfires. Prepare the practical details in advance so that when they are willing, nothing takes more than an hour to arrange.

05Do you offer intervention services or referrals?

Ask whether it is in house and what it costs. Interventions are a specialized skill and a bad one causes lasting damage.

06How do I know if this is an emergency?

Withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines, any overdose, and any suicidal statement are emergencies. Call 911 or 988 rather than an admissions line.

07What is the visiting or contact policy during the program?

Ask specifically, and ask whether it changes by phase.

08How do I keep naloxone in the house, and how do I use it?

Available without a prescription in New Jersey. Ask the center for training if opioids are involved.

09What does relapse mean for the treatment plan?

Ask whether the person can return and how quickly. Relapse is common and a program's response to it tells you its model.

10What support exists for me, not just for them?

Al-Anon, Nar-Anon and family therapy are different things. Ask which the center actually connects you to.

Your care compass · Aftercare & alumni

Ten questions before you choose

For the phase most people underestimate. Nearly every program describes itself as offering aftercare; far fewer can tell you what specifically exists ninety days after discharge, who runs it, and whether it costs anything.

01What exists at ninety days after discharge, specifically?

Not what is offered in week one. Ask about month three, when the structure is gone and the risk is highest.

02Is aftercare included in the program cost or billed separately?

Frequently separate. Ask before you finish the program, not after.

03Who runs the alumni program, and how often does it meet?

Ask for a schedule. An alumni program that meets when someone remembers to organize it is a mailing list.

04Do you offer sober living, and is it yours or a partner's?

IKON states it does not provide sober living in house. Ask who owns the house, who staffs it, and what the rules are.

05Will I keep the same therapist when I step down a level?

Continuity of relationship is one of the strongest predictors of staying engaged. Ask directly.

06What is the process for coming back if I relapse?

Ask how fast, and whether readmission requires starting the intake process from the beginning.

07Do you track outcomes, and will you tell me what they are?

Very few centers will answer this honestly. The answer, including a refusal, is informative.

08Is there recovery coaching or peer support, and is it credentialed?

Certified peer recovery specialists are a recognized credential in New Jersey. Ask whether the coaches hold it.

09How do you help with the practical things after discharge?

Employment, housing, legal issues, transportation. Case management either exists or it does not.

10What happens to my medication management after the program ends?

Ask who prescribes at month four, and whether they can help you find someone.
