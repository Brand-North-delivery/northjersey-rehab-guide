A claim that does not check out

North Jersey Recovery Center publishes this on its own website:

No state license number published. No accreditation published

Neither licensure nor accreditation is published anywhere on the site.

What this does and does not mean

We could not establish that North Jersey Recovery Center is unlicensed, and nothing here should be read that way. A facility can hold a perfectly valid New Jersey license and still publish a wrong sentence about it — treatment websites are routinely built from recycled templates, and a licensing line copied from an out-of-state original is the most likely explanation.

What we can say is narrower and still matters: the sentence gives a reader nothing they can check. A licensing claim exists so that a family can verify it at 11pm before making a phone call. One that names a regulator in the wrong state cannot be verified by anyone, which is why our algorithm scores it at zero rather than at face value.

What to do about it

  1. Ask North Jersey Recovery Center directly for its New Jersey DMHAS license number and the name on the license.
  2. Check that number against the New Jersey Substance Abuse Monitoring System treatment directory.
  3. Ask which entity holds the license, and whether it is the same entity operating the Fair Lawn, NJ address.

Quoted from the center’s own website in August 2026. New Jersey licenses substance use disorder treatment through the Division of Mental Health and Addiction Services in the Department of Human Services, and through the Department of Health; it has no Department of Health Care Services. If this has since been corrected, we will update it — the claim, not the center, is what is being assessed.
